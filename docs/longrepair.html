Did training on longer repairs pay off?

On 8,192 fresh weeks the new agent earned 0.687 more credits per week than the earlier closure agent. On four- and five-half-day repairs the difference was +1.146 [+0.770, +1.539] credits/week. Fresh earnings against the rolling planner and constant penalty were both inconclusive. The matched short-training agent earned more on the fresh weeks. The release met 2 of the five registered targets.

How the weeks are scored

Every dispatcher sees the same freight, closure warning and repair updates on a given week. Each has its own truck positions. Net credits deduct loaded travel, empty travel and missed-contract penalties. On-time service counts the share of ten due contracts served, including contracts interrupted by a closure.

The release designated training seed 13 before scoring. Its five tests compare earnings and on-time service separately against the rolling planner and the constant penalty. A win in earnings requires a positive lower bound on its paired 95% interval. Matching service requires a zero or positive lower bound. The fifth test checks the original double-return schedule on new ordinary weeks. Each paired 95% interval resamples entire weeks within the same freight schedule. Service differences are measured in percentage points (pp).

The new agent learns from simulated pairs of moves on the same freight. During fitting, it estimates how net credits and contracts served change by Friday if it takes the earlier agent’s suggestion. At dispatch time it uses the current board, elapsed closure and public repair updates. When the two existing dispatchers agree, it takes their shared move.
