Did the new policy catch more?

The new policy caught a larger share of profitable switches. The release found 98 of 151 profitable moves on the same fresh opportunities where the previous detector found 19. The recall difference was +52.32 [+44.30, +60.36] percentage points.

The release switched on 190 of 316 boards, up from 30. Its forecasts ranked profitable moves ahead of other moves about as often as before (AUROC 0.601 versus 0.600). Of the 160 additional long choices, 79 wins earned 2,190 extra credits, 51 losses cost 1,793 credits and 30 choices tied. Together these moves changed Friday earnings by +397 credits and deliveries by +33 contracts.

Earnings against always short, the constant penalty and the planner were all inconclusive. The release met 4 of eight registered tests.

How the policy handles previously skipped moves

On a board the old rule would skip, the release searches its fit data for the 64 most similar public freight and repair situations. It estimates the win probability, extra credits, downside and service change from their measured Friday outcomes. The training used 1,627 new paired decisions across 180,224 fitting weeks. Both policies protect the predecessor’s long choice whenever they see the same board.

On a previously skipped move, switching requires an estimated win probability of at least 30.0% and a score strictly above 0 credits. Start with expected net credits, add 12 credits per additional contract expected on time and subtract 0.10 times the expected downside. The rule was selected on 90,112 separate weeks.

Calibration set the release’s direct earnings slope to zero. It predicted +0.691 extra credits on every previously skipped board. The predicted win probability, service change and downside varied across boards and determined which additional moves qualified.

The control that replaces the learned forecasts with calibration averages takes every previously skipped long move. It follows the long-trained expert on all disagreements. The release earned -0.014 [-0.042, +0.014] credits/week relative to that control, with an on-time difference of +0.002 [-0.001, +0.006] percentage points.

How the weeks are scored

Every dispatcher sees the same freight, closure warning and repair updates on a given week. Each has its own truck positions. Net credits deduct loaded travel, empty travel and missed-contract penalties. On-time service counts the share of ten due contracts served, including contracts interrupted by a closure. A repair lasts one to five half-days.

The study designates seed 13 before scoring. One registered test compares recall with the previous detector on the same decisions. Six compare weekly earnings and on-time service with short, the rolling planner and the constant penalty. A final test checks earnings against the planner on fresh double-return weeks. Positive gains in earnings or recall require a positive lower paired 95% bound. The service target requires the mean and its lower bound to reach zero percentage points or higher.

Both policies start from two frozen dispatchers. One fitted on short depot repairs and the other also saw longer repairs. When they suggest different moves, the new policy can keep short or switch to long. The real closure length and future loads stay hidden when the fleet dispatches.
