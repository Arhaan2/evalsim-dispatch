Did RL beat both dispatchers?

The constant penalty earned 0.344 more credits per closure week than RL. The earnings comparison with the planner was inconclusive. On 8,192 fresh weeks, the fleet earned 219.50 net credits per week and delivered 58.07% of contracts on time. 2 of the five registered tests passed.

How often did RL change the dispatch?

Across 34,229 boards with a closure warning or active repair, the benchmark recorded 23,459 eligible choices and 808 changes from the constant-penalty move on the release trajectory. The two dispatchers’ action sequences differed on 687 full weeks. Weekly comparisons use each dispatcher’s own truck positions.

How the weeks are scored

Every dispatcher sees the same freight, closure warning and repair updates on a given week. Each has its own truck positions. Net credits deduct loaded travel, empty travel and missed-contract penalties. On-time service counts the share of ten due contracts served, including contracts interrupted by a closure.

The release tests earnings and on-time service separately against the rolling planner and the constant penalty. A win in earnings requires a positive lower bound on its paired 95% interval. Matching service requires a zero or positive lower bound. The fifth test asks whether new ordinary weeks on the original double-return schedule retain an earnings win over the planner.
