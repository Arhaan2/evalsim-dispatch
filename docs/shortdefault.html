What did switching add?

The release followed the short-trained default on every board. Against that default it earned exactly zero additional credits. On 16,384 fresh weeks, the release switched on 0 of 153 disagreements. On 10,938 weeks with four- or five-half-day repairs, the difference against always following short was 0.000 [0.000, 0.000] credits/week.

Before opening the test bank, seed 13 tried twelve switching rules across 22,528 selection weeks. None qualified. Each needed a positive earnings gain over short after subtracting one standard error and on-time service at least as high. The frozen policy always chose short. How the weeks are scored

Every dispatcher sees the same freight, closure warning and repair updates on a given week. Each has its own truck positions. Net credits deduct loaded travel, empty travel and missed-contract penalties. On-time service counts the share of ten due contracts served, including contracts interrupted by a closure. A repair lasts one to five half-days.

The release designated training seed 13 before scoring. The study tests earnings and on-time service separately against the rolling planner and the constant penalty, and measures the incremental change over the short-trained default. A win in earnings requires a positive lower bound on its paired 95% interval. Matching service requires a zero or positive lower bound. The fifth test checks the original double-return schedule on new ordinary weeks. Each paired 95% interval resamples entire weeks within the same freight schedule. Service differences are measured in percentage points (pp).

During training, the agent tries both moves on identical freight and estimates how switching changes earnings and contracts served by Friday. During dispatch, the agent sees the current board and public repair notices. It follows the short-trained agent when the two policies agree.
