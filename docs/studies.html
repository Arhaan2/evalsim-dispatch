When is a survey worth buying?

Trace the decisions in reverse publication order. Net earnings include any fee the agent paid. Each study has its own fixed shifts and controls.

When to stop waiting
On cold reversals the agent beat the planner by 0.28 credits per shift. Its 50 paid surveys booked the same loads as skipping and cost 3.75 credits in total. On hot drops, stopping eight profitable waits cost 80 credits against the previous freight rule at the same fees.
Choosing the final load
Among paid purchases, 101 hot-drop load choices changed, with 64 paying more and 37 paying less. Neither fresh target showed a reliable gain over always skipping. The original interrupted-drop gains held.
One slot left
The specialist beat the planner on fresh hot drops and cold reversals. The two cold-reversal purchases added zero gross freight and cost 0.15 credits in total.
Variable prices
The agent made 264 paid purchases on fresh shifts. Its differences from the price-aware planner on both target endings had intervals that included zero.
A one-credit survey
Both the trained agent and planner skipped all 8,192 fresh surveys. Forcing the agent to buy reduced mean net earnings by 1.04 credits.
Booking before the survey
Learning when to reserve capacity gained 0.26 credits per cold reversal over the distribution-aware planner and cost 1.39 per hot drop.
Misleading bulletins
The survey agent beat the planner by 0.84 credits per cold reversal and trailed by 1.04 per hot drop. It missed the original-drop retention target.

Can the agent recognize a demand turn?

A brief rally and a lasting reversal can look the same at first. These studies test how much the history of signed offers can tell a dispatcher.

Brief rallies
The agent earned 0.64 credits more than the planner across fresh shifts. Both cold-ending reversals and hot-ending drops favored the planner.
Routing to a planner
Choosing between three policies recovered the historical interrupted-drop gains. The mean fresh reversal difference from the planner was −0.02 credits, with a 95% interval from −0.23 to +0.19.
Hot and cold endings
On fresh reversals, using history added 0.70 credits over the no-history agent on cold endings and cost 0.47 on hot endings.
Demand turns
The Turns agent recovered 2.49 credits per reversal over the earlier Drift policy, while the planner still led by 0.69.
Shifting demand
On patterns held out of training, the original Drift learner beat the planner by 11.46 credits on interrupted drops and lost 12.10 on reversals.

What should the dispatcher do next?

The original environments measure the consequences of reserving capacity, inspecting stock, moving goods and selecting freight tools.

Freight Reserve
On 768 test shifts the Q-learning agent earned 33.70 credits per shift, close to 33.71 for the planner that used the full distribution of offers.
Inventory Cutoff
Trained policies and the observation-only planner completed all 192 held-out shifts. Disabling the planner’s inventory check reduced completions to 144.
Meridian Harbor
Uniform reinforcement learning delivered every order on time on 82.5% of held-out shifts, averaged across three runs. A later planner completed all 168 shifts.
Language-model decisions
In the decision-level training experiment, the Qwen adapter completed 4 of 21 fresh freight shifts. The base model completed 2 and the dispatcher completed 17.
