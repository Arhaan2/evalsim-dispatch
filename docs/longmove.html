Did the detector earn its place?

The fleet found 5 of 72 profitable moves on the short-trained dispatcher’s path. The probability scores ranked profitable moves above nonwins more often than chance on this sample. AUROC was 0.617 [0.528, 0.702]. Across 16,384 complete fresh weeks, switching earned +63 total credits relative to always following short (+0.004 [-0.004, +0.013] credits/week). The earnings comparisons against short, the constant penalty and the rolling planner were all inconclusive. The release met 3 of seven registered tests.

Which profitable moves did the detector find?

Probability estimates and switching decisions

The release trained on 90,112 new simulated weeks. Its 7,451 paired training decisions included 3,309 where switching earned more. The fitting decisions include alternate starting truck positions and place extra weight on positions reached by the short-trained fleet. The 62 profitable choices from the earlier study stayed outside fitting, calibration, selection and testing.

The frozen rule switches only when the estimated win probability is at least 40.0% and expected earnings exceed 5 additional credits. Its selected rule placed no extra credit value on service. The weekly benchmark scores on-time deliveries separately.

AUROC measures how often the raw detector ranks a profitable move above an unprofitable one, with half credit for a tied prediction. The Brier score measures how far each forecast probability was from what happened, giving larger errors more weight. Lower is better. The constant forecast uses the training calibration bank’s win rate (47.2%). Precision and recall measure switches that cleared every dispatch requirement. Precision is undefined when the rule never switches.

How the weeks are scored

Every dispatcher sees the same freight, closure warning and repair updates on a given week. Each has its own truck positions. Net credits deduct loaded travel, empty travel and missed-contract penalties. On-time service counts the share of ten due contracts served, including contracts interrupted by a closure. A repair lasts one to five half-days.

Seed 13 was designated for release before scoring. Six fresh-week tests measure earnings and on-time service against the short-trained default, the rolling planner and the constant penalty. A seventh tests earnings on fresh double-return weeks. Earnings wins require a positive lower paired 95% bound. Matching service requires both the point estimate and the lower bound to reach zero percentage points or higher. Intervals resample entire weeks within freight schedule.

The short-trained dispatcher fitted its policy on repairs lasting one to three half-days. The long-trained dispatcher also fitted on repairs lasting four or five half-days. When the two policies agree, the fleet takes their shared move. During a disagreement, the detector uses the public freight board and repair notice. During fitting, paired simulator runs supply the labels for four boosted-tree models. They estimate the probability of a win, the size of a gain or loss and the effect on service. At test time only the evaluator can try both choices on identical future freight.
